How bits are counted

Each word's information is its surprisal under the model: bits = −log₂ p(word | text before it). A predictable word like “of” costs a fraction of a bit; a surprising word can cost 10 or more. The human share credits each word to whoever decided it.

Each part is scored given everything before it, so ideas from a reply make your later text cheaper. “Human share including the chat” = (your text bits + plan + your messages) ÷ (all of those + AI replies).
Plan measurementsaved = bits(text, no plan) − bits(text | plan)“Used” = saved ÷ plan bits: how much of the plan's information ended up in the text.

With the class server, the model sees a chat: your plan is the request and your text is the reply. Predictions and scores come from Prof. Arnold's writing engine (llm.thoughtful-ai.com), which reads your typing as characters, so half-typed words are handled on the server, and offers whole words with the model's own probabilities. An option with several words counts its choice bits once, on its first word. Rescore the exported logs with score_sessions.py so every condition is measured by the same model.
